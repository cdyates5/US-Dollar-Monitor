US External Position & the Dollar

The Gourinchas–Rey external-adjustment channel: a cyclical measure of the US net external position — net foreign assets plus net exports, detrended — that historically led the dollar, because external imbalances must eventually close through the exchange rate. The headline finding here is not a signal but a regime change: the channel was powerful into the 1990s and has decayed toward zero in the reserve-currency era.

The centrepiece: a fading lead

Rolling 40-quarter correlation between the external-imbalance measure and the subsequent 4-quarter dollar move. Positive means the classical channel is working — a weak external position precedes depreciation. It ran near +0.8 in the 1980s and has collapsed to roughly zero over the past decade, even dipping negative. This is the "exorbitant privilege" signature: in the reserve-currency era the dollar has strengthened despite a deteriorating external position, because it trades on global safe-asset and reserve demand rather than trade adjustment.

The backdrop it fights

The secular deterioration in the raw net position: NIIP has fallen from a small surplus to roughly −67% of GDP. The classical channel says this "should" weigh on the dollar; the safe-asset era has overridden it.

Components

The two ingredients of the imbalance, each detrended and standardised so positive = stronger external position. The stock (net foreign assets) and the flow (net exports) each carry part of the signal; combined they beat either alone.
