Relative Real Narrow Money & the Dollar

The NS Partners / Simon Ward specification — six-month annualised growth of real (CPI-deflated) narrow money, US minus a DXY-weighted foreign basket — tested as a dollar lead. This is the third relative-money specification put through the gauntlet, after nominal broad M2 differentials and global M2. The verdict does not change.

Real narrow money momentum: US, foreign, and the gap

Six-month annualised growth of real M1-family money. US = currency + demand deposits (M1A — immune to the May-2020 M1 redefinition), deflated by CPI. Foreign = euro area M1 and Japan M1 at DXY weights, deflated by HICP / CPI. DXY YoY on the right axis. The gap is currently historically wide — and the profile below shows why that isn't a trade.

Lead profiles — the gap vs its parts

Correlation with DXY YoY at horizon k, 1997–2026 (the window where both foreign legs are properly deflated). The gap is flat and era-unstable. What little signal exists sits in the foreign side, inverted — weak foreign real money preceding a firmer dollar — the same "smile, not the gap" structure the Lead Index found in growth data.
