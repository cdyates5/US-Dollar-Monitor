Real Yield Curve → US Dollar

Two widely-circulated charts claim the US real yield curve leads DXY by twelve months. We reconstruct both from source data and stress-test the lead — measuring where the predictive power actually sits rather than accepting the advertised lag.

Construction

All series are month-end, 2003–present (TIPS availability). "Advanced / pushed forward k months" means the signal at time t is aligned with DXY YoY at t+k — i.e. the signal leads.

The absolute level of the 10s–3m curve is sensitive to the short-rate deflator. Trailing CPI YoY is used because it reproduces the source chart's ~800bps 2022 peak; ex-ante deflators (Cleveland Fed 1Y expected inflation) compress the level to ~+250bps but leave the turning points — and therefore the lead conclusion — essentially unchanged.

Validation

Cross-correlation across leads 0–24m identifies the empirically optimal lead; a rolling 60m window tests regime stability; an OLS of DXY YoY on the curve at the optimal lead gives the predictive fit and live read.

Verdict

Spec A A real relationship, correctly signed, but the "12-month lead" is marketing. Predictive power sits on a 6–9 month plateau (peak ≈8m, r≈+0.34) and has faded to r≈+0.22 by month 12. Even at its best it explains ~11% of DXY YoY variance — a directional tilt, not a timing tool.

Spec B Does not hold. At the advertised 12-month lead the correlation is +0.08 — indistinguishable from zero. The genuine information is coincident and inverted (r≈−0.31): a steepening real 2s10s tends to accompany a weaker dollar in the same month, not a stronger one a year later. The apparent lead is an overfit to the 2020–23 cycle.

Live The real 10s–3m curve has re-steepened to — (short real rates falling faster than long as disinflation proceeds), implying — DXY YoY roughly eight months out — mild firmness, the opposite of the "more downside to go" the 2023 vintage carried.
