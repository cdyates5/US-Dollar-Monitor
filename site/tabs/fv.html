US Dollar Fair Value

A change-on-change model: the 12-month move in the ICE Dollar Index regressed on the contemporaneous 12-month change in its priced fundamental drivers — the 10-year rate differential, relative equity performance, oil and volatility. Fair value compounds the fundamentals-implied move; the gap is the residual. A diagnostic of what the dollar should have done, not a timing signal.

The misvaluation gap

Actual 12m change minus the fundamentals-implied 12m change (the regression residual). Orange = dollar rich vs its drivers, teal = cheap. Dashed guides at ±1σ and ±2σ. The gap is highly persistent (half-life ≈ 6 months) but — stated plainly — it has no predictive correlation with forward dollar returns; historically it closes through fundamentals catching up, not the dollar converging.

Gap → forward returns

Mean 6m-forward DXY change by gap bucket, 1991–2026. Non-monotonic — the rich/cheap reading does not time the dollar; it tells you what is priced.

Model stability

Rolling 10-year R² of the 12m-change regression. The fundamentals share of dollar variance is regime-dependent — strongest in the QE and post-COVID eras.

Coefficients

Full-sample OLS on 12m changes, Newey–West (12 lags) t-statistics. Signs are conditional: with rates in the model, relative equity behaves as a risk-appetite proxy and oil as the contemporaneous reflation channel.

History

Last 24 months. Gap σ is the residual scaled by its full-sample standard deviation.
