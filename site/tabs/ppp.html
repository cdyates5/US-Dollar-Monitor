The dollar's decade:
PPP overvaluation as a 10-year lead

The real (CPI-adjusted) trade-weighted dollar reverts toward its long-run average, so how far it sits above fair value today has historically foreshadowed its return over the following ten years. On this DXY-basket model the dollar is currently rich, and the relationship — while statistically strong — rests on only a handful of independent decades. Read the forecast as a slow gravitational pull, not a timing signal.

Construction & honest caveats

The signal. Real DXY = published DXY × (US CPI ÷ trade-weighted foreign CPI), with the DXY basket weights (EUR 57.6, JPY 13.6, GBP 11.9, CAD 9.1, SEK 4.2, CHF 3.6) applied geometrically. Overvaluation is its % deviation from the full-sample geometric mean — the relative-PPP notion that the real exchange rate is stationary around a constant, so distance from that constant measures rich/cheap.

Data. DXY from ICE (daily, month-end). CPI from IMF IFS for all seven economies (German CPI carries the euro leg, spliced through the Deutsche-Mark era for the bilateral panel). Nominal DXY runs to Jul 2026; the CPI basket is current to Jul 2025 and held flat thereafter — immaterial for a decade-scale anchor.

Overlapping windows overstate significance. Ten-year forward returns sampled monthly share 119 of 120 months with their neighbours. The Newey–West t is corrected for this, but the honest denominator is ~4–5 non-overlapping decades since 1971. This is a slow structural regularity seen a few times, not a high-frequency edge.

Fair value is not fixed. The "long-run average" uses the whole sample, which peeks at the future. Re-running the anchor on an expanding, backward-only window keeps the result intact (R² 0.70, implied −2.7%/yr) — reassuring, but the level of "fair value" still drifts with the window you choose.

PPP is a decade anchor, not a trade. Rich currencies stay rich for years (the dollar was ~30–60% "overvalued" for much of the 1980s and 2000s before turning). Valuation tells you the terminal pull and the odds, not the entry.
